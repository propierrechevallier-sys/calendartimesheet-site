Google Calendar Time Insights: where it is, why it's missing

Updated 19 October 2026. About 6 minutes to read.

Time Insights is the panel in Google Calendar that shows how your time splits between meetings. Many people look for it and cannot find it. Three reasons explain almost every case: you use a personal Google account, your company's Google Workspace edition does not include it, or you are on your phone. Google's own help page offers Time Insights for Google Calendar on a work or school account. A personal Google account, the free one that comes with Gmail, does not have it, and no setting turns it on.

Where to find Time Insights

  1. Open Google Calendar in a browser on a computer.
  2. On the left side, click More insights.
  3. Choose the period with the calendar view: week view for a week, month view for a month.
  4. Hover a section of a chart: the matching meetings are highlighted in your calendar, in the same colour.

If there is no More insights on the left, one of the cases in why Time Insights is not showing applies.

Which accounts have Time Insights

Google's admin help lists the Google Workspace editions that support it: Business Plus, Enterprise Standard, Enterprise Plus, Education Fundamentals, Education Standard, Education Plus and Nonprofits (Turn Time Insights on or off for users, read on 19 October 2026). Business Starter and Business Standard are not on that list, and neither are personal Google accounts. A small company on Business Standard therefore does not see Time Insights, even with every setting switched on.

What Time Insights shows

  • Time breakdown: focus time, one-to-one meetings, meetings with three or more guests, invitations you still need to answer, and the time that remains. The remaining time appears only if you have set your working hours.
  • Time in meetings: an average over the previous three weeks, or the previous three months in month view.
  • People you meet with: the five people you spend the most time with in the date range. You can pin up to 10 people to follow them.

The date range follows the calendar view: switch to week or month view to change it. On work or school accounts you can also give events labels, up to 200, and follow them in the time breakdown.

One privacy point from Google's help: anyone who can make changes and manage sharing on your calendar can also see your Time Insights.

What it does not count

Time Insights is built to understand meetings, not to bill hours. For time in meetings, Google counts an event only when you accepted it, it has at least one other guest, it is marked busy and it lasts less than 8 hours. When two meetings overlap, the shared time is counted once. A work block you schedule alone, such as "Acme: draft report", is therefore not a meeting for Time Insights, even if it is the work you invoice.

The help page also describes no export, no total per client and no hourly rate. Time Insights answers "where did my week go?"; it does not produce the list of hours a client expects at the end of the month.

Alternatives on any Google account

Free, in your browser: the hours calculator

Export your calendar from Google Calendar (Settings, Import & export, Export) and drop the .zip into the free Google Calendar hours calculator. It adds up the hours per calendar, per keyword in the title and per week, for the period you choose, solo work blocks included. It works with personal accounts and every Workspace edition, needs no sign-in, and reads the file in your browser: nothing is uploaded.

Free: a short script and a pivot table

Copy your events into Google Sheets with the ten-line script in the guide to turning Google Calendar into a timesheet: one line per event with date, start, end, hours, title and colour. Then follow how to count hours in Google Calendar per client to add them up by colour, by word in the title or with a pivot table. It takes an hour to set up and a few minutes each month to rerun.

Automatic: Calendar Timesheet

Calendar Timesheet works in Google Sheets with personal Google accounts as well as work accounts. It reads the calendars you tick and writes one line per event in your own spreadsheet; with autopilot, it does so every hour. Solo work blocks count as much as meetings. Each event is matched to a client by colour, calendar, a word in the title or the guest's email domain.
