How to count hours in Google Calendar per client, colour or week

Updated 17 October 2026. About 7 minutes to read.

Google Calendar shows when you work, not how much. There is no total per client, per project or per colour in a personal Google account. Counting hours takes two things: a consistent way to say which client each meeting belongs to, and a place where the durations are added up and split. This guide is about the split: per client, per colour, per week or per month. For a single total, see Can Google Calendar calculate hours worked?

Step 1: tag each meeting with its client

There are four common conventions. Pick the one you will actually keep up.

ConventionGood forWatch out for
A colour per clientFew clients, visual planning. Set it with right-click on an event.Google offers 11 event colours; past 11 clients, colours run out.
A calendar per clientMany clients, or sharing a client's calendar with them.More calendars to tick and keep visible.
A word in the title, like "Acme: weekly call"Any number of clients, works on the phone.One typo and the meeting goes uncounted.
The guest's email domain, like anyone at acme.comClient meetings with invitations: no tagging at all.Internal work without guests needs another tag.

Mixing is fine as long as the rules are clear: for example, colour first, then the guest domain for meetings you forgot to colour.

Step 2: add up the durations per client or colour

Inside Google Calendar (work accounts only)

With some Google Workspace business, enterprise and education editions, the Time insights panel shows how your time splits, including by colour. It shows charts, not a list you can invoice from, and it has no rates. Personal accounts do not have it: the guide to Time Insights on a personal account explains what it counts and what to use instead.

In your browser, for a one-off count

Export your calendar from Google Calendar and drop the file into the free Google Calendar hours calculator: it totals the hours by calendar, by keyword in the title and by week, without uploading anything. Google's export has no event colours, so for totals by colour use one of the methods below.

In Google Sheets, for free

Get one line per event into a sheet first. The guide to turning Google Calendar into a timesheet has a ten-line script that writes date (column A), start, end, hours (column D), title (column E) and colour number (column F). Then:

  • By colour: =SUMIF(F:F, "10", D:D) adds the hours of colour 10. Google numbers event colours 1 Lavender, 2 Sage, 3 Grape, 4 Flamingo, 5 Banana, 6 Tangerine, 7 Peacock, 8 Graphite, 9 Blueberry, 10 Basil, 11 Tomato; an empty value means the calendar's own colour.
  • By word in the title: =SUMIF(E:E, "Acme*", D:D) adds every event whose title starts with Acme.
  • All clients at once: select the table, then Insert, Pivot table, with Colour or a client column in rows and the sum of Hours in values.

To turn colours into client names, add a small two-column table (colour number, client) and a VLOOKUP next to each line.

Hours per week and per month

Add one helper column (G) next to the lines, then sum or pivot on it.

  • Per week, Monday to Sunday: =ISOWEEKNUM(A2) in column G, then =SUMIF(G:G, 42, D:D) for the hours of week 42.
  • Per month: =TEXT(A2, "yyyy-mm") in column G, then =SUMIF(G:G, "2026-10", D:D) for October 2026.
  • Per client and per month: with the month column above, a pivot table with the client in rows, the month in columns and the sum of Hours in values.

Week numbers restart every January, so for a list that spans two years, sum between two dates instead: =SUMIFS(D:D, A:A, ">="&DATE(2026,10,5), A:A, "<"&DATE(2026,10,12)) adds the week that starts on Monday 5 October 2026.

Automatically, with Calendar Timesheet

Calendar Timesheet reads your calendars every hour and applies your rules in order: colour, calendar, title word, guest domain. Meetings that match no rule wait in a To sort list, where one click assigns them and can remember the choice for next time. Totals per client, amounts and a dashboard update on their own, and each month closes into one statement per client.

Make the count match reality

  • Use what happened, not what was planned. Move or shorten the event after the meeting if it ran differently. A calendar is only as true as its last edit.
  • Separate done from planned. Mid-month, hours already worked and hours still booked are different numbers. Both are useful: one for the invoice, one for the forecast.
  • Skip what is not work. Declined invitations, all-day events and time marked free should not count.
  • Count shared meetings once. A meeting copied into two of your calendars is still one hour.
