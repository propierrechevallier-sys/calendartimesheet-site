Can Google Calendar calculate hours worked?

Updated 15 October 2026. About 5 minutes to read.

No. Google Calendar places events on a grid, but it never adds up their durations, and there is no total at the bottom of a week. The setting called Working hours does not count anything either. To know how many hours you worked, the durations have to be added up somewhere else: in your head, in Google Sheets, or by a tool that reads the calendar for you.

Working hours is availability, not a count

On a work or school account, Working hours tells other people when you are available, so that they book meetings at sensible times. Google describes it as a recurring weekly schedule: Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00, for example. It says nothing about what you actually did. A day with three hours of client work and a day with ten both show the same working hours.

Method 1: read the hours off the calendar

For a handful of events, week view is enough: add the blocks one by one and write the total down. Past ten events, or as soon as you need a total per client, this becomes slow and easy to get wrong.

Method 2: Google Sheets formulas

Put one event per line, with the date in column A, the start time in column B and the end time in column C. Google Sheets stores a time as a fraction of a day, which explains the formulas below.

You wantFormula in column D
Hours as a decimal number (1.5 for 1 h 30)=(C2-B2)*24, with column D formatted as a number
An event that ends after midnight=MOD(C2-B2,1)*24
Rounded up to the next quarter hour=CEILING((C2-B2)*24,0.25)
The total of the column=SUM(D2:D200)

If you prefer to keep durations as times (1:30 rather than 1.5), use =C2-B2 instead, and format the total with the custom number format [h]:mm (Format, Number, Custom number format). Without the square brackets, a total of 26 hours is shown as 2:00, because the display wraps every 24 hours.

The formulas need the events in the sheet first. The timesheet guide has a short script that copies them for any period, and the template guide adds minimum charges, amounts and monthly totals per client.

Method 3: let a tool read the calendar

Calendar Timesheet does the copying and the arithmetic in your own Google Sheets: one line per event with date, start, end and hours, then totals per client, matched by colour, calendar, a word in the title or the guest's email domain. With autopilot, the sheet updates every hour and closes each month on its own.

Four things that make the total wrong

  • All-day events. A holiday or a deadline marked all day is not 24 hours of work. Leave them out.
  • Declined invitations. They stay on many calendars. Count only what you attended.
  • The same meeting in two calendars. Copied or shared events can appear twice. Count each one once.
  • What was planned instead of what happened. If a call ran 20 minutes over, move the end of the event before you count.
