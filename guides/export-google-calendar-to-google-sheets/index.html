Export Google Calendar to Google Sheets automatically

Updated 19 October 2026. About 6 minutes to read.

Google Calendar has no "export to Sheets" button. What it offers is an export of your calendars as .ics files, the standard calendar format, meant for moving events into another calendar app. Getting events into a spreadsheet, and keeping them there up to date, takes one of the approaches below. If all you need is a total of hours, the free Google Calendar hours calculator reads Google's export directly, with no spreadsheet at all.

Option 1: Google Calendar's own export

In Google Calendar, open Settings, then Import & export, then Export. You download a zip with one .ics file per calendar (Google's help page describes it).

  • Good: complete, official, works for any account.
  • Not so good: Google Sheets cannot open .ics files, so you need a converter. Many write one line per event series, with the repeat rule instead of the actual dates; the free Google Calendar to Excel and CSV converter on this site writes one line per occurrence, and its .xlsx file imports into Google Sheets (File, Import). It is still a snapshot: next week, you do it again.

If the file has to end up in Excel or as a CSV, see how to export Google Calendar to Excel or CSV, with durations.

Option 2: a script with a timer

Apps Script, built into Google Sheets, can read your calendar and write rows, and a time-driven trigger can run it every hour. Start from the short script in the guide to turning Google Calendar into a timesheet, then, in the Apps Script editor, open Triggers, click Add Trigger, choose your function, "Time-driven" and "Hour timer".

  • Good: free, exactly what you want, stays in your account.
  • Not so good: you maintain it. Re-running it rewrites the sheet, so notes you add next to the rows get misaligned. Reading several calendars, skipping declined invitations, splitting meetings across midnight and avoiding duplicates are all yours to write.

Option 3: an IFTTT applet

IFTTT links two services without code. Its applet "Add new Google Calendar events to Google Sheets" fires every time a new event is added to your calendar and adds one row at the bottom of the first worksheet of a spreadsheet you choose. The applet page gives two limits: on IFTTT's free plan, the calendar is checked every hour (every 5 minutes on paid plans), and a new spreadsheet is created after 2000 rows.

  • Good: free, no code, runs on its own once connected.
  • Not so good: it reacts to new events only. Events that already exist are not copied, and the action only adds rows, so a meeting you move or cancel later keeps the line written when it was created. Durations and totals per client are yours to add with formulas. Connectors such as Zapier or Make work the same way; check what their free plans include.

Option 4: an add-on for Google Sheets

Add-ons install into Google Sheets in two clicks and do the reading for you. They fall into two families:

  • Raw exporters copy events into a sheet, often with a schedule. They suit people who want the data and will build their own reports.
  • Timesheet add-ons go further: they turn events into hours per client or project, with totals. Calendar Timesheet is one of them, and it is ours.

Before installing any add-on, look at what Google asks you to allow. A tool that only reads should ask to view your calendars, not to edit them, and to access only the spreadsheet you use it in.
